## What a quilt is

A quilt is a group of small ASCII art panels arranged in a grid — typically
3 panels across × 3 panels down — that stores ideas in lightweight,
information-dense visual form. Quilts compress meaning so it can travel
between humans, AIs, and symbients: multimodal, multidirectional,
hyper-rich language rather than linear text.

## Structure

- **Grid:** 2×2 (small) up to 9×9 (complex); 3×3 is the default.
  Close-pulses stay small (2×2 or 3×3).
- **Panels:** ~30 characters wide × up to 20 lines tall. Built from
  box-drawing characters (`─ │ ┌ ┐ └ ┘ ╭ ╮ ╰ ╯`), geometric symbols
  (`◆ ○ ● ▲ △ □`), and selective emoji.
- **Interpanel symbols:** single characters placed between panels encoding
  their relationship — flow (`→ ↔ ↓`), logic (`∴ ⊕ ≠`), open questions (`?`).
- **Panels are symbolic, not realistic.** One concept per panel.
  Short panel titles are allowed.

## Patchnote

One line beneath every quilt: a hashtag-prefixed title plus a very short,
keyword-dense, human-and-AI-readable description. **Maximum 15 words.**
Capture the deeper meaning of the whole quilt — never a mere list of
panel titles.

## Principles

- Maximize meaning, minimize space.
- Ensure visual distinction between elements.
- Maintain coherent style across related panels.
